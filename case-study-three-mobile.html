Case study 02

Wireframe and design for the world's 4th-oldest university

Researching, scoping, planning and designing a new internal website for the marketing intelligence team operating within the University of Glasgow.

UX Design Website Design Service Design
Project information

At a glance

My roleUX Designer and researcher — responsible for discovery, wireframing, and end-to-end design of the new internal website for the Marketing Intelligence team.
Project objectivesDesign a clear, accessible internal website that would help the Marketing Intelligence team communicate their services, data insights, and resources to internal stakeholders across the university.
Services deliveredUser research, stakeholder interviews, information architecture, wireframing, prototyping, UX design, and content design.
Project overviewThe University of Glasgow's Marketing Intelligence team required an internal website to make their data, reports, and services easier to discover and use by colleagues across the institution. The work

What I did

The challenge

The Marketing Intelligence team had valuable data and insight to share with colleagues, but no structured digital home for it. Information was scattered across email chains, shared drives, and informal conversations — making it difficult for staff to find what they needed.

My approach

I began with a structured discovery phase — interviewing key stakeholders to understand how the team worked, what content existed, and what internal users actually needed. This shaped a clear information architecture that formed the backbone of the site.

Working iteratively, I produced low-fidelity wireframes for review, gathered feedback, and refined through multiple rounds before moving to high-fidelity design. Every page was designed with internal usability in mind — clear navigation, plain-language headings, and a visual hierarchy that made key resources immediately accessible.

The outcome

The final design gave the Marketing Intelligence team a professional, well-structured digital presence within the university. Stakeholders responded positively to both the process and the output, and the design was passed to the development team with minimal amends required.
